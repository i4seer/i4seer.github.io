From Seer Frights #1

The Swamp Glow

Mrs. Calhoun's green pucks that sit in the mud and glow like something alive. Three versions: one that takes ten minutes, one that takes a weekend, one takes a career.

⚠ DON'T BUILD ALONE ⚠

These instructions involve electronics and tools that may cause injury. Adult supervision is required. Use at your own risk.

See full Safety & Legal Notice and Terms of Use.

Version 1 — The Easy One

No Soldering. No Wiring. Ten Minutes.

If you can use tape, you can make this.

What You Need

  • 1 green LED, diffused (the soft kind — not the bright clear ones. Craft store or online. About a quarter.)
  • 1 CR2032 coin battery (the flat silver one from the drugstore) — OR 1 AAA battery if you can't find coin batteries. AAAs are in every junk drawer in America. The glow will be softer, which is actually better. Won't fit in a golf ball, so go bigger — a tennis-ball-sized ornament or a plastic Easter egg.
  • 1 tiny slide switch (optional — or just pull the battery out to turn it off)
  • Electrical tape
  • 1 plastic ornament ball from the craft store (the kind that splits in half — clear, round, about the size of a golf ball)
  • Aquarium silicone (optional — makes it waterproof. Skip this if you're not putting it in water.)
  • Green glass paint (optional — one coat makes it look swampy. Without it, the clear ball still glows green from the LED inside.)
  • Fine sandpaper (optional — roughing up the ball makes the glow softer. But it works without it.)

How to Build It

The LED has two legs — one long, one short. The long leg is positive. The short leg is negative. Sandwich the coin battery between them — long leg on the flat side (+), short leg on the bumpy side (−). It lights up. That's it. That's the whole circuit.

Wrap a piece of electrical tape around the battery and the legs to hold it together. If you want a switch, tape the slide switch between the long leg and the battery — slide on, it glows. Slide off, it stops.

Tuck the whole thing inside half the ornament ball. LED facing out. Close the ball. If you want it waterproof, seal the seam with aquarium silicone and let it cure overnight. Sand the outside so it's frosted. One coat of green glass paint. Let dry.

Water safety: Even sealed housings can leak. Water and batteries don't mix — keep battery connections dry. Retrieve devices after use. Do not leave unattended in water overnight.

You now have a glowing green swamp puck. Total cost: about a dollar. Total time: ten minutes. Total soldering: zero. It won't cycle on and off — it just glows steady until the battery dies (a few days). But in the dark, sitting on a tree root or dropped in shallow water, it looks exactly the same. Spooky enough.
Version 2 — The Timer Build

CMOS 7555 Timer. Slow Pulse. Loops Forever.

This is what Mrs. Calhoun actually uses. Requires soldering and careful wiring.

Low-voltage build. Use 2x AAA batteries (3V total). Do not connect to household electrical wiring.

What You Need

CMOS 7555 timer (ICM7555 or TLC555, 8-pin DIP) Qty: 1
Small piece of perfboard Qty: 1
Green 5mm diffused LED Qty: 1
Resistor, 330Ω (LED current limit) Qty: 1
Resistor, 10kΩ (R1) Qty: 1
Resistor, 2.2MΩ (R2) Qty: 1
Electrolytic capacitor, 470µF (timing capacitor) Qty: 1
2× AAA battery holder (3V total) Qty: 1
Small slide switch Qty: 1
Hookup wire 1 spool

Pin Orientation

Hold the chip so the small notch faces left.

Bottom-left is Pin 1. Top-left is Pin 8. Pins count counter-clockwise.

Pin Reference

  • Pin 1 — Ground
  • Pin 2 — Trigger
  • Pin 3 — Output
  • Pin 4 — Reset
  • Pin 5 — Control (leave unused)
  • Pin 6 — Threshold
  • Pin 7 — Discharge
  • Pin 8 — VCC (+3V)

Step 1 — Mount the Chip

Solder the 7555 to the perfboard. Keep the notch facing left for reference. Trim excess leads.

Step 2 — Power Connections

  • Solder Pin 1 to battery negative (ground).
  • Solder Pin 8 to battery positive (+3V).
  • Connect Pin 4 to Pin 8 (+3V).
  • Leave Pin 5 unconnected.

Step 3 — Create the Timing Loop (Astable Mode)

  • Connect Pin 2 to Pin 6 (solder a short jumper between them).
  • Solder the 10kΩ resistor (R1) between Pin 7 and Pin 8 (+3V).
  • Solder the 2.2MΩ resistor (R2) between Pin 7 and the Pin 2/6 junction.
  • Solder the 470µF capacitor between the Pin 2/6 junction and Pin 1 (ground).

Important: The capacitor stripe (negative side) goes to ground (Pin 1).

Step 4 — Connect the LED

  • Solder the 330Ω resistor to Pin 3 (Output).
  • Connect the other end of the 330Ω resistor to the long leg of the LED.
  • Connect the short leg of the LED to Pin 1 (ground).

Step 5 — Add the Switch

Insert the slide switch between the battery positive lead and Pin 8. Slide ON to power the circuit.

How It Works

The capacitor slowly charges through the resistors. When it reaches a threshold, the chip switches states and discharges the capacitor. This repeats automatically.

The large 2.2MΩ resistor and 470µF capacitor slow the timing, creating a long, breathing pulse.

Testing

Insert batteries and turn the switch on.

The LED should slowly cycle on and off.

If it does not, disconnect power and re-check wiring. Nothing should become hot.

Housing

Mount the perfboard and battery pack inside the ornament housing. Keep battery connections dry and seal carefully if placing near moisture.

Water safety: Even sealed housings can leak. Water exposure still carries risks to electronics and batteries. Retrieve devices after use and inspect seals regularly.

Once it works, it feels less like a circuit and more like a small machine that breathes.
Version 3 — Go Further

Tick. ATOM Matrix. Control from Your Phone.

This is the upgrade. A tiny computer with a 5×5 color LED grid, Wi-Fi, and an accelerometer — running Tick. Everything runs on the device itself. No server. No internet. Just the puck and your phone.

What You Need

ATOM Matrix — 24mm square, 5×5 RGB LED matrix, Wi-Fi, accelerometer. About $15. Qty: 1
USB-C cable Qty: 1
Small waterproof power bank (or long USB cable to a hidden outlet) Qty: 1
Same housing as Version 1 & 2 — ornament ball, silicone, sandpaper, green glass paint —

Parts — where to buy each one →

How to Build It

Step 1 — Plug it in. Connect USB-C to power. The firmware is already on the ATOM Matrix — no flashing, no setup tools. It boots in 2 seconds and starts glowing with default settings.

Step 2 — Configure it. Hold the button for 3 seconds. The device becomes a Wi-Fi hotspot. Connect with your phone and open 192.168.4.1. A setup page loads right from the device. Set the color to swamp green. Dial the brightness down. Program a slow pulse — two hours on, ten minutes dark. Save. Done. Walk away. It runs on its own.

Step 3 — Wi-Fi (optional). If you’ve got a network nearby — your home Wi-Fi, a hotspot, anything — enter the credentials on the setup page. Now you can control every puck from any browser on the network. Change patterns from inside the house. No need to walk out.

Step 4 — Seal it. Same as Version 1 & 2. Tuck the ATOM Matrix inside an ornament ball, LED grid facing out. Run the USB-C cable out through a sealed hole to a battery pack hidden in the roots. Aquarium silicone the seam. Sand it. Green glass paint. Let dry.

Tick is self-contained. Everything runs on the device — LED patterns, timing, accelerometer. No server required. Configure from the setup page in the field. Add Wi-Fi when you want browser control from inside the house. Total cost: about $15 per puck plus a battery pack. No soldering. No timer chip. No wiring.

Where to Place Them

On stakes in the mud. Wedged into tree roots. Sitting on stumps. Drop them right into shallow water — the housing holds tight enough to sit submerged for hours. Those ones make the water itself glow from underneath. Looks like something's down there, looking up.


Version 3: Same placement, but the USB cable needs to reach a battery pack. Tuck the power bank into a waterproof bag and bury it in the mud next to the roots. Or use a long USB cable run to a hidden outlet if you’re close enough to the house.


The glow should look alive. Not electronic. Not a flashlight. Alive. Like the swamp is watching you back. If it looks like a gadget, you did it wrong. Sand the shell. Rough it up. Let it sit in the mud for a day. It should look like something you found, not something you bought.
