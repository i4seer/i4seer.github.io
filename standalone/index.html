Standalone novels

One book. One story.

Not every B. Carter novel belongs to a series. These are grounded, character-driven books that stand entirely on their own—no reading order, no shared world, no series wordmark. Just one story, start to finish.

Historical mystery

The River Run

A missing case. A missing man. One family's truth.

Sugar Island, 1929. Eighteen-year-old Will Hart knows boats, engines, and the river his family uses to move whiskey past Prohibition. When his uncle disappears the same night a case goes missing, Will runs his own investigation against the one family he can't afford to be wrong about.

Coming-of-age

The County Fair

Leaving and choosing are not the same thing.

Michigan, summer 1987. An eighteen-year-old farm boy takes temporary work with a traveling midway crew, thinking freedom just means being able to leave. A summer of mechanical work and an unhurried romance teach him that staying can be its own kind of freedom, once it's finally his own decision.

Why standalone?

Some stories don't need a series.

Seer Worlds, Journey, Seer Frights, and Seer Warns each build a larger world across books. These novels do the opposite on purpose: one setting, one cast, one complete arc, closed by the last page. Read one without needing to read another.
