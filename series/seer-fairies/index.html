Seer Fairies

Every fairy is real. The rules were forgotten.

Ancient European fae followed immigrants to America, carried inside the trades and traditions that once kept them managed. The factories closed. The mines sealed. The grandmothers died without telling anyone. The Seer is the last compact-keeper—working one case at a time in towns that no longer know what they're living next to.

Case 01 · Hollow Creek, WV

The Púca

It cannot lie. It can only ask the question you've been refusing to answer.

Something with gold eyes is watching the crossroads in Hollow Creek. Eli Conner, twelve, rides through the junction every day in his father's too-big hoodie. The Púca cannot lie—but it can ask the one question Eli has been refusing to answer for two years.

Case 02 · Dryden, KS

The Sluagh

The host does not depart. The host settles.

Six months after her grandmother's death, the Whelan farm is leaking away—cattle, equipment, a family's quiet paralysis nobody will name. A flock of grey figures crosses the stubble field at sunset, carrying the faces of the unmourned dead. The defense isn't iron. It's what Helen left unsaid.

Case 03 · Millbrook, TN

The Each-Uisge

They are not two things.

Sixty feet down in the Millbrook reservoir, a drowned town still stands. Wren Calloway rows out every morning to the spot where her brother drowned two years ago and was never recovered. Then a black horse appears in the shallows—and a boy with damp hair and no recorded road. The bridle is the weakness.

How the series works

Every case stands alone. The migration mystery doesn't.

Each book is a complete, standalone case: one entity, one town, one cost. Read them in any order. Underneath the cases runs a larger mystery—the compact that once managed the fae, and why it broke—that only fully comes together for readers who follow the Seer across the series.
