From Seer Frights #3

The Three-Knock Device

Garrett Hargrave's piezo knocker that taps three times through a cabin door. Three versions: manual, auto-tick, and the real setup.

⚠ DON'T BUILD ALONE ⚠

These instructions involve electronics and tools that may cause injury. Adult supervision is required. Use at your own risk.

See full Safety & Legal Notice and Terms of Use.

Version 1 — The Easy One

Press a Button. Make a Knock.

No wiring. No programming. If you can press a button, you can make this.

What You Need

  • 1 piezo disc, 27mm (the flat brass kind — looks like a tiny gold coin with two wires. About the size of a quarter. Electronics store or online. About a dime.)
  • 1 AAA battery (from the junk drawer, the TV remote, wherever)
  • 1 tactile push button, 6x6mm (the tiny clicky kind you see inside electronics — four little legs, about the size of a pencil eraser. You can buy a bag of 100 for a couple bucks online. You only need one.)
  • Electrical tape

Parts — where to buy each one →

How to Build It

The piezo disc has two wires — one red, one black. Tape one wire to one end of the battery. Tape the other wire to one leg of the push button. Run a short wire from the other leg of the button to the other end of the battery. That's the whole circuit.

Tape the disc flat against a door, a wall, a bunk frame — any surface that carries sound. Run the button wire far enough that you can stand around the corner. Press the button — tick. Press it three times: tick, tick, tick. Through wood, it sounds like knocking.

That's Version 1. You are the timer. You press three times and walk away. Total cost: about a quarter. Total skill required: can you press a button? You're done.
Version 2 — Slightly Less Simple

Hands-Free. It Ticks on Its Own.

What You Need

Piezo disc, 27mm (same as above) Qty: 1
AAA battery Qty: 1
Blinking LED (blinks on its own — no chip needed) Qty: 1
Push button (push on, push off) Qty: 1
Electrical tape 1 roll

Parts — where to buy each one →

How to Build It

Wire the blinking LED in series with the piezo disc and the battery, with the push button in the circuit. Press the button to close the circuit. The LED pulses on and off automatically. Each pulse sends a tick through the piezo. Tape the disc to a door and it sounds like a faint, steady tapping. Tick... tick... tick...

You can't control "three knocks then silence" with this one. It just ticks steady until you press the button again to kill it. But tape a ticking disc behind someone's door frame at night and tell me that's not creepy enough.

Total cost: about fifty cents. Total skill: tape things together, press a button.

Low-voltage build. Use 1x AAA battery (1.5V). Do not connect to household electrical wiring.

Version 3 — Go Further

Tick. ATOM Matrix. Atomic Echo Base. 3:07 AM. Every Night.

This is what Hargrave actually uses in the book. Tick devices with Atomic Echo Bases hidden behind door frames, a Wi-Fi network, and a timer set for 3:07 AM. Three knocks. Every cabin. Same time. Same spacing. Every single night.

What You Need

ATOM Matrix — 24mm square, 5×5 RGB LED matrix, Wi-Fi. About $15. Qty: 1
Atomic Echo Base (speaker + microphone board) Qty: 1
USB-C cable Qty: 1
USB power source (wall adapter or battery pack) Qty: 1

Parts — where to buy each one →

The Setup

The ATOM Matrix plugs into the Atomic Echo Base — a speaker and microphone board. He recorded the knock sound himself — his knuckle on a pine door, three times, slow and even. Each device connects to the camp Wi-Fi, syncs time via NTP, and waits.

At 3:07 AM every device plays the audio through the Echo Base speaker. Every cabin hears the same three knocks at the same time. The 5×5 LED grid flashes amber with each tap — but nobody sees that. The device is hidden.

That's the part that really gets them — not the knocking itself, but the fact that it's synchronized. Raccoons don't knock on eight doors at the same time.

"I'm not writing the wiring details here. There aren't any. ATOM Matrix plugs into the Atomic Echo Base. USB power. Connect to Wi-Fi. Done. If you're smart enough to find this note, you're smart enough to set one up. And if you're one of those campers — well. You already did." — G.H.

Where to Place Them

Versions 1 & 2: Tape the disc flat against the inside of a door, behind a bed frame, under a windowsill. Any surface that vibrates. Wood is best. Metal works. Drywall is fine. The disc is thin enough to hide behind a poster, under a shelf, inside a drawer.


Version 3: Behind door frames, under porch steps, inside the gap between the roof and the cabin wall. The stacked boards are tiny enough to hide behind a shelf or wedge into a gap. Speaker facing the door. USB cable runs to any outlet or battery pack. Anywhere a counselor wouldn't think to look. Anywhere that makes the sound come from the door itself.


The knock should sound real. Not a buzz. Not a beep. A knock. Heavy. Patient. The kind of knock that says I know you're in there. I can wait.

If it sounds electronic, you did it wrong. If it sounds like an insect, try a bigger surface. If it sounds like knocking — congratulations. You just ruined someone's night.
