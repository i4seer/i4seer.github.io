From Seer Frights #2

The Route 13

A pressure strip hidden under a seat. Someone sits down — and the device knows. Three versions: dead simple, timed, and the real setup.

⚠ DON'T BUILD ALONE ⚠

These instructions involve electronics and tools that may cause injury. Adult supervision is required. Use at your own risk.

See full Safety & Legal Notice and Terms of Use.

Version 1 — The Easy One

Step On It. It Lights Up. That's It.

No soldering. No wiring diagrams. If you can tape two things together, you can make this.

What You Need

  • 1 pressure mat switch (the flat, flexible kind — used in door chimes and Halloween props. Two thin strips that touch when you step on them. About the size of a welcome mat. Under $2 online.)
  • 1 orange LED, diffused (the soft kind, not the bright clear ones. Craft store or online. About a quarter.)
  • 1 CR2032 coin battery (the flat silver one from the drugstore)
  • Electrical tape

Parts — where to buy each one →

How to Build It

The pressure mat has two wires. The LED has two legs — one long (positive), one short (negative). Wire the mat in series with the LED and the battery. Long LED leg to battery positive (+). Short LED leg to one mat wire. Other mat wire to battery negative (−). Wrap the connections with electrical tape.

Slide the mat under a chair cushion, a doormat, a rug — anywhere someone will step or sit. Run the LED wire to wherever you want the glow to appear. When weight hits the mat, the circuit closes and the LED lights up orange. Lift the weight, it goes dark.

That's Version 1. Someone sits down, a light turns on somewhere. That's all it takes. Total cost: about $3. Total time: five minutes. Total soldering: zero. Simple, cheap, and creepy.
Version 2 — The Timer Build

Step On It. It Pulses. Then Fades.

Same pressure mat, but now the LED pulses slowly for 30 seconds after someone sits down — then fades to dark. Requires soldering, a few small parts, and an adult who knows what a capacitor is.

Low-voltage build. Use 2x AAA batteries (3V total). Do not connect to household electrical wiring.

What You Need

Pressure mat switch (same as Version 1) Qty: 1
Orange LED, 5mm, diffused Qty: 1
555 timer chip (classic 8-pin DIP) Qty: 1
Capacitor, 100µF electrolytic (sets the fade time) Qty: 1
Resistor, 560kΩ (sets the 30-second fade) Qty: 1
Resistor, 100Ω (protects the LED) Qty: 1
2× AAA batteries + holder Qty: 1
Small perfboard, hookup wire, solder 1 ea.

How It Works

When the pressure mat closes the circuit, the 555 fires in monostable mode. The 560K resistor and 100µF capacitor set the pulse duration to about 30 seconds. The LED lights up bright, then slowly fades as the capacitor drains. Even after the person gets up, the LED keeps glowing for a while — like the seat remembers.

How to Build It

Step 1 — The chip. The 555 has 8 pins. Hold it so the notch faces left. Pin 8 goes to battery positive (+). Pin 1 goes to battery negative (−). That's power.

Step 2 — The trigger. Connect one wire from the pressure mat to pin 2 (trigger). The other mat wire goes to battery negative (−). Add a 10K pull-up resistor between pin 2 and battery positive. When the mat is pressed, pin 2 gets pulled LOW and the 555 fires.

Step 3 — The timing. Solder the 560K resistor between pin 7 and battery positive. Solder the 100µF capacitor between pin 6 and battery negative (watch polarity — stripe side to negative). Connect pin 6 to pin 7 through the resistor path. This sets the ~30 second on-time.

Step 4 — The LED. Connect the LED (with its 100 ohm resistor) from pin 3 (output) to battery negative. When the 555 fires, pin 3 goes HIGH and the LED lights up.

Step 5 — Housing. Solder everything to a small piece of perfboard. Tuck it inside a small project box or tape it behind furniture. Run the pressure mat wire to wherever you're hiding the mat.

Takes about thirty minutes once you've done it before. The first one will take a couple of hours. The fade effect is worth it — someone sits down, the light glows, they get up, and it keeps glowing. Like the seat is still warm.
Version 3 — Go Further

Tick. ATOM Matrix. The Bus Knows You Sat Down.

This is the upgrade. A pressure mat wired to a tiny computer with a 5×5 color LED grid. Someone sits down — the grid lights up orange, shows "13", and counts passengers. Everything runs on the device. No server. No internet.

What You Need

ATOM Matrix — 24mm square, 5×5 RGB LED matrix, Wi-Fi, accelerometer. About $15. Qty: 1
Pressure mat switch (same as Versions 1 & 2) Qty: 1
USB-C cable Qty: 1
USB power source (wall adapter, power bank, or hidden outlet) Qty: 1
2 short jumper wires + 1 resistor, 10KΩ (mat to GPIO pin) 1 set

Parts — where to buy each one →

How to Build It

Step 1 — Plug it in. Connect USB-C to power. The firmware is already on the ATOM Matrix — no flashing, no setup tools. It boots in 2 seconds and shows a dim orange idle pattern.

Step 2 — Wire the mat. The pressure mat has two wires. One goes to GPIO 32 on the ATOM Matrix. The other goes to GND. A 10K pull-up resistor connects GPIO 32 to 3.3V. That's it — three wires and one resistor. When the mat is pressed, GPIO 32 reads LOW and the firmware triggers.

Step 3 — Configure it. Hold the button for 3 seconds. The device becomes a Wi-Fi hotspot. Connect with your phone and open 192.168.4.1. A setup page loads right from the device. Set the trigger color, brightness, display pattern ("13" or passenger count), and how long the display stays lit after someone gets up. Save. Done.

Step 4 — Hide it. Tuck the ATOM Matrix behind furniture, under a shelf, inside a gap — LED grid facing out where people can see the glow. Run the pressure mat under a cushion, doormat, or rug. Run the USB cable to a hidden power source.

Tick handles everything — pressure detection, LED patterns, timing, passenger counting. Configure from the setup page. Add Wi-Fi when you want browser control from another room. Total cost: about $17. No soldering required for the ATOM Matrix itself — just two jumper wires and a resistor to the mat.

Where to Place Them

The mat: Under a doormat. Under a chair cushion. Under a rug at the top of the stairs. Under the seat of a porch swing. Anywhere someone will step or sit without looking down. The mat is thin and flexible — they won't feel it through a cushion.


The device: Hidden nearby. Behind a table leg, under a shelf, inside a drawer with a gap. The LED grid needs to face outward so the glow is visible, but the device itself should be out of sight. Run the mat wire along the baseboard or under the rug.


Version 3: The ATOM Matrix is 24mm square. It fits behind almost anything. The USB cable needs to reach power — a battery pack tucked behind furniture, or a long cable to a hidden outlet. The pressure mat wire runs to the GPIO pin on the device.


The glow should feel wrong. Not a flashlight. Not a night light. A dim orange glow that appears when someone sits down — and stays just long enough to make them wonder. If it looks like a gadget, you did it wrong. If it looks like the room just noticed you — you got it right.
